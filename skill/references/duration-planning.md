# Duration-aware academic talks

Use the speaking time to choose the depth of explanation, structure and compression. The same paper should become a different argument at different lengths, with its central claim and evidence boundaries intact. These are planning anchors, not fixed slide counts or empirical timing rules.

## Take in the time

At intake, ask for the paper (PDF or link) and duration if missing. Accept minutes, a range or a split such as “20 minutes plus 5 minutes Q&A”. Reuse supplied inputs and begin reading as soon as the paper arrives. Settle the timed structure once the duration is known. Confirm any missing presenter name or school after sharing the structure and before finalizing the cover. Continue working on the body and speaker notes while waiting. If the user explicitly asks to proceed without a duration, choose and disclose a provisional 20-minute speaking plan.

Account for time consistently:

- “20-minute talk”: plan 20 minutes of speaking; say Q&A is outside that budget unless the user states otherwise.
- “30-minute slot including 5-minute Q&A”: plan 25 minutes speaking + 5 minutes Q&A. Embedded discussion and demonstrations also consume the stated slot.
- “15–20 minutes”: build a coherent 15-minute core and identify up to 5 minutes of optional expansion with clear re-entry points.
- When an inclusive slot has no Q&A allocation, make the split explicit and easy to change; do not silently spend the whole slot twice. A very short slot or conflicting constraints may need one clarification.

## Explain the level before making slides

Provide a compact plan in the user's language: **speaking/Q&A budget → audience learning goal → depth and compression → timed structure → main omissions/backup**. This is a visible production decision, not a new approval gate. Then proceed to creation when the necessary inputs are known.

| Representative speaking time | Depth and learning goal | Structure and summarization |
|---|---|---|
| About 3–5 min | Contribution snapshot: what changed, why it matters, what supports it | One problem, one idea, a minimal causal mechanism, the decisive evidence and its key limit. Merge background into the example; summarize mechanisms by operations rather than module lists. |
| About 8–12 min | Focused overview: how the central idea works | Minimal baseline, system overview, one worked path, main comparison and a bounded conclusion. Keep one useful diagnostic if time and source evidence allow. |
| About 15–25 min | Standard research talk: understand and assess the contribution | Necessary background, baseline difference, stepwise mechanism with a stable example, main evidence, the most relevant diagnostic and limits. Summarize secondary variants; keep essential settings visible. |
| About 30–40 min | Technical explanation: inspect assumptions and design choices | More mechanism dependencies, selected implementation/theory details, complementary evidence, cost/failure analysis where reported, and a focused discussion. |
| About 45–60+ min | Deep seminar: reason about the method and its boundaries | Detailed mechanism or derivation, sourced alternatives, several evidence questions, limitations and discussion with time reserved. Broader context requires an actual audience goal and sources; duration alone does not justify a survey. |

Use the nearest anchor as a starting point. Adapt intermediate lengths and audience expertise continuously; a minute across a row boundary should not radically change the talk. A long talk on a thin source can use careful case walkthroughs and clearly labeled discussion, or recommend a shorter presentation; it cannot invent results or related work.

## What changes when the talk gets shorter

Preserve **problem → changed mechanism → supporting evidence → scope**. Compress by selecting and restructuring, not by accelerating speech or shrinking text:

1. Remove duplicate examples and background without downstream dependencies.
2. Move secondary variants, tables and implementation details to backup.
3. Keep one complete mechanism path; summarize internal detail without creating false arrows or collapsing important conditions.
4. Choose the most decisive evidence and any condition or counterevidence that could change its interpretation. Essential costs/risks stay in the main argument even when other diagnostics are removed.
5. Narrow the claim when its necessary explanation or evidence cannot fit. Keep prerequisites the actual audience needs; cut topic breadth instead of making the mechanism unintelligible.

A short talk still requires reading and checking the sources needed for its claims. Fewer spoken details do not permit weaker verification, invented numbers, or hidden limitations. Longer talks add understanding, not text density.

## Make a timed outline

Budget sections from their explanation burden: definitions, visual reading time, transitions, worked examples, demonstrations and planned interaction. Estimate each page/reveal from what must be said and understood; derive slide count afterward. A title slide and a dense experiment figure do not take equal time. Label timing as estimated until rehearsed, and leave reasonable slack within the slot.

Example for a 20-minute speaking plan, with Q&A separate:

| Time | Purpose |
|---|---|
| 0–2 | Problem and learning goal |
| 2–4 | Necessary background and baseline |
| 4–5 | Key change |
| 5–11 | Mechanism through one example |
| 11–17 | Main evidence and one relevant diagnostic |
| 17–20 | Conclusion, decisive limit and a short transition to questions |

This is an editable starting budget, not a universal proportion. Reallocate time to the paper's actual difficulty and the audience. If the source lacks a diagnostic, do not fabricate one to fill the slot.

When the user changes duration, revise the learning goal, main/bridge/backup decisions, mechanism detail, evidence selection, discussion time, notes and timed outline together. Keep a coherent ending at the shorter duration; optional extensions must not contain facts necessary to interpret the core conclusion.

## Completion check

State the selected depth and why it fits. The estimated speaking, transitions, embedded interaction and external Q&A must fit the declared slot without double counting. Distinguish omitted spoken detail from unverified source material. Keep the timed outline and optional cuts with the storyboard; report actual rehearsal timing only when it was performed.
